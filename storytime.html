Library of Doom

  • Delta Green: Denied to the Enemy by Dennis Detwiller

    Set during World War II, this novel explores the early, bloody days of Delta Green as they race against the Nazi occult organization Karotechia to secure unnatural and terrifying cosmic powers.

  • The Laundry Files by Charles Stross

    A bit more bureaucratic and darkly comedic, focusing on a secret British intelligence agency dealing with Lovecraftian horrors, occult math, and world-ending entities.

  • Delta Green: The Rules of Engagement by John Scott Tynes

    An agent's disappearance pulls a Delta Green team into a vortex of horror in this techno-thriller novel. The secrets they uncover threaten to ignite an all-out war between the Delta Green conspiracy and its bitterest rival government faction, Majestic-12. (Note: This is often published as part of the "Strange Authorities" collection).

  • The Joe Ledger Series by Jonathan Maberry

    Follows a detective who gets recruited into a secret government tier called the Department of Military Sciences (DMS) to fight weaponized paranormal threats, including Lovecraftian monsters.

  • Delta Green: Through a Glass, Darkly by Dennis Detwiller

    A direct continuation of the overarching plotline, this novel explores the violent collapse of the secretive Majestic-12 organization and the rise of the new, modernized Delta Green as hidden truths come to light.

  • SEAL Team 666 by Weston Ochse

    Exactly what it sounds like—Navy SEALs tasked with taking down demons, cults, and supernatural monsters.

  • Delta Green: Alien Intelligence Edited by Bob Kruger and John Tynes

    The original collection of short stories that kicked off the fiction line, featuring various authors exploring the intersection of 1990s government cover-ups, rogue operations, and Lovecraftian horror.

  • Delta Green: Dark Theatres Edited by John Tynes

    The second anthology of short stories, expanding the universe with more tales of intelligence agencies and military personnel confronting unspeakable cosmic threats that regular citizens are blissfully unaware of.

  • Delta Green: Strange Authorities by John Scott Tynes

    A collection that compiles Tynes' interconnected short stories (like The Corn King) along with his full-length novel The Rules of Engagement into one cohesive, espionage-heavy volume.

  • Delta Green: Tales from Failed Anatomies by Dennis Detwiller

    A haunting, highly praised collection of short stories written entirely by one of the game's co-creators, covering the massive sweep of the conspiracy's dark history from the 1920s into the near future.

  • Delta Green: Extraordinary Renditions Edited by Shane Ivey

    A collection of short stories written by various authors that pushes the Delta Green timeline forward into the modern, post-9/11 War on Terror era of the 2010s.

  • Delta Green: The Way It Went Down (Volumes 1 & 2) by Dennis Detwiller

    Collections of very brief, rapid-fire vignettes and flash-fiction stories detailing the grim, clinical, and often fatal day-to-day operations of Delta Green agents.

  • The works of Clark Ashton Smith Various

    He was a close pen-pal of Lovecraft and wrote the Hyperborean cycle, a series of stories set in a doomed, prehistoric, magic-filled continent (Hyperborea). His style was more poetic, heavily fantasy-driven, and slightly more sardonic than Lovecraft's. He also created the popular toad-god "Tsathoggua," which Lovecraft loved so much he borrowed it for his own Cthulhu Mythos.

  • The works of Robert E. Howard Various

    Best known for creating Conan the Barbarian, Howard was the third pillar of the Weird Tales "Big Three" alongside Lovecraft and Smith. He frequently corresponded with Lovecraft and contributed several core elements to the early Cthulhu Mythos, often blending cosmic horror with his signature pulse-pounding, pulp-action sword and sorcery.

  • The works of August Derleth Various

    While not part of the original "Big Three," Derleth is the author most famously inspired by Lovecraft. In fact, he is the one who actually coined the term "Cthulhu Mythos" (Lovecraft himself jokingly called it "Yog-Sothothery"). After Lovecraft died, Derleth founded a publishing company specifically to keep his friend's work in print and wrote dozens of his own stories expanding on Lovecraft's universe.

  • The works of Robert Bloch Various

    He eventually became famous for writing the novel Psycho, but he started his career as a teenager writing Cthulhu Mythos stories as Lovecraft's protégé!

Public Domain Works

Because Robert E. Howard and Clark Ashton Smith were highly prolific pulp authors in the 1920s and 1930s, a massive portion of their catalogs is now in the public domain in the United States.

As of 2026, any work published before January 1, 1931, has automatically entered the public domain. Additionally, because pulp magazines like Weird Tales or the authors' estates frequently failed to renew copyright registrations for stories published between 1931 and 1963, many of their later works are also free to use.

Here is a breakdown of their most notable public domain works:

Robert E. Howard

Howard’s estate controls the trademarks for branding purposes (e.g., you can't just slap the "Conan" logo on a new comic book without permission), but the actual text of his original stories is largely in the public domain.

Conan the Barbarian:

Nearly all of Howard's original Conan stories are public domain because their copyrights were never renewed. You can find them freely available on Project Gutenberg. Highlights include:

  • The Hour of the Dragon (Howard's only full-length Conan novel)
  • Red Nails
  • Queen of the Black Coast
  • Beyond the Black River
  • The People of the Black Circle
  • Shadows in the Moonlight
  • A Witch Shall Be Born

Solomon Kane:

  • "Red Shadows" (1928)
  • "Skulls in the Stars" (1929)
  • "Rattle of Bones" (1929)

Kull of Atlantis:

  • "The Shadow Kingdom" (1929)
  • "The Mirrors of Tuzun Thune" (1929)

Other Standalone Fiction:

  • People of the Dark (1932)
  • Black Canaan (1936)
  • "Spear and Fang" (1925 — Howard's first published work)

Clark Ashton Smith

Smith has a slightly more complex copyright situation because the publisher Arkham House (and later Smith's estate) did successfully renew the rights to several of his later story collections. However, his early works and many of his Weird Tales submissions are entirely in the public domain.

Poetry Collections (Fully Public Domain):

Before writing pulp fiction, Smith was a celebrated poet. His early, highly regarded cosmic and romantic poetry collections are entirely public domain:

  • The Star-Treader and Other Poems (1912)
  • Odes and Sonnets (1918)
  • Ebony and Crystal: Poems in Verse and Prose (1922)
  • Sandalwood (1925)

Short Stories & Weird Tales:

Any of his short stories published prior to 1931 are public domain, along with several later stories that lapsed due to non-renewal (such as his famous Hyperborean tale, "The Seven Geases"). Some notable public domain stories include:

  • "The Ninth Skeleton" (1928)
  • "The Last Incantation" (1930)
  • "Sadastor" (1930)
  • "The Phantoms of the Fire" (1930)
  • "The Uncharted Isle" (1930)
  • "The Seven Geases" (1934 - lapsed)

A Quick Warning on Collections:

If you are looking to read or use these works, always look for the original magazine text (like the Weird Tales scans). Modern publishers often restore draft texts, fix typos, or add introductions to their specific anthologies, and those specific edits and additions are protected by modern copyright, even if the underlying story is not. Websites like Project Gutenberg or The Eldritch Dark are excellent resources for finding the verified public domain versions of these texts.
